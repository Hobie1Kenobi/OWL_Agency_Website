AI legal assistants still need a verification layer

March 24, 2025

Lists of “top AI legal assistants” usually compare drafting speed, integrations, and subscription price. Those are product features. They are not evidence that a cited case exists or that it stands for the proposition in the draft.

OWL is not in that category. OWL is a verification layer you run on work those tools (or a person) already produced. Try a free citation check on a real public example, Carpenter v. United States, 585 U.S. 946 (2018), or paste your own cite.

When you evaluate an assistant, ask: does it show what was checked, against what source, and where a human still has to look? If the product only returns fluent prose, you still need a separate verification step. OWL does not claim unsourced time-saved or accuracy percentages, and it does not replace the attorney who signs the brief.

Failure states matter. A useful verifier says citation not found, ambiguous citation, or source unavailable — it does not fail silently.
