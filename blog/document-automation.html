Document automation still needs citation verification

March 15, 2025

Document automation is useful when it reduces retyping of captions, certificates, and repeating clauses. It is a production tool. It is not a substitute for checking whether the authorities in that draft exist, resolve, and actually support the sentence they sit under.

OWL is a verification layer on that output. Paste a citation — or a paragraph that contains citations — on /verify and run the five checks: existence, citation format, holding support, verification path, and a human review flag. The first check is free. No account.

A fluent automated memo can still cite a case that is not in any reporter, or a real case that does not stand for the proposition. Courts treat that as the lawyer’s error. Automation that cannot show what was checked, against what source, is incomplete for filing work.

If you automate document assembly, put verification at the end of the same workflow: generate, then verify, then a person reviews. OWL does not replace a lawyer, a paralegal, or a research database. Human review of every output is required.
