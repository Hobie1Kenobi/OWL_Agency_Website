How citation verification works

Legal AI should not just answer. It should verify. OWL sits on top of research you already have and shows the trail before a cite is filed.

How to verify AI-generated legal citations

OWL is a verification layer. It is not an AI lawyer, not a paralegal replacement, and not a Westlaw competitor. It checks whether a cited authority can be confirmed against public legal sources so a human reviewer can decide what to file. Verification is not legal advice.

The public working example is Carpenter v. United States, 585 U.S. 946 (2018). You can paste that cite, or any other paragraph that contains citations, on /verify. No signup for the first check. The filing-side method is in Verifying AI-generated citations before filing.

How OWL verifies a citation

Five checks, in order. Human review of all outputs is required. Verification is not legal advice.

  1. Existence

    Does this case exist in the reporter?

  2. Citation format

    Is the cite well-formed and does it resolve?

  3. Holding support

    Does the case stand for the proposition?

  4. Verification path

    What was checked, against what source?

  5. Human review flag

    Where does a person still have to look?

Failure is explicit: citation not found, ambiguous citation, or source unavailable. OWL does not publish unsourced accuracy percentages.

What a verification audit trail contains

The trail records the pasted string, which of the five checks ran, which public source was used, and whether the result was pass, fail, or needs review. It is not a chatbot screenshot. Read What a verification audit trail should contain.
