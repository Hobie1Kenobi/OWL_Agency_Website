How citation verification works

Legal AI should not just answer. It should verify. OWL sits on top of research you already have and shows the trail before a cite is filed.

Verify a citation free See pricing

A layer, not a replacement

OWL is a verification layer. It is not an AI lawyer, not a paralegal replacement, and not a Westlaw competitor. It checks whether a cited authority can be confirmed against public legal sources so a human reviewer can decide what to file.

The public working example is Carpenter v. United States, 585 U.S. 946 (2018). You can paste that cite, or any other paragraph that contains citations, on /verify. No signup for the first check.

The five checks

Existence

Does this case exist in the reporter?

Citation format

Is the cite well-formed and does it resolve?

Holding support

Does the case stand for the proposition?

Verification path

What was checked, against what source?

Human review flag

Where does a person still have to look?

Failure is explicit: citation not found, ambiguous citation, or source unavailable. OWL does not publish unsourced accuracy percentages.

Questions

Does OWL replace a lawyer or a research database?

No. Human review of all outputs is required. OWL does not create an attorney-client relationship and is not legal advice.

What does a verification check include?

The five checks above, plus a visible path from the pasted string to the public source used in that run.

How is this priced?

Free for one citation check. Per-document, firm (including the published $6,000/month figure with an audit log), and enterprise/API are on /pricing. Value is framed against the cost of one bad citation, not hours saved.
