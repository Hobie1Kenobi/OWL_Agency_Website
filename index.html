Legal AI should not just answer. It should verify.

AI research tools surface cases that do not exist, or that do not say what they are cited for, and the lawyer who signs the brief carries that risk.

Verify a citation free See how it works

The problem is accountability, not answers

Generative research tools can return fluent memos that cite authorities which do not exist in any reporter, or which exist but do not stand for the proposition they are used to support. Courts treat those errors as the lawyer’s. Telling a tribunal that a model produced the citation is not a defense. The person who signs the brief is accountable for every authority in it.

The five checks

OWL is a verification layer on top of research you already have. It does not replace a lawyer, a paralegal, or a research database. It checks citations so a human reviewer can see the trail before filing.

Existence

Does this case actually exist in the reporter?

Citation format

Is the citation well-formed and does it resolve?

Holding support

Does the case actually stand for what it is being cited for?

Verification path

Can you see and follow the chain from claim to source?

Human review flag

Where does OWL say a person needs to look at this?
